## What this submission contains

The report designs a twelve-month security awareness program for about 800 staff across head office, warehouses, and remote sales. It follows the lifecycle in the course materials: assess, plan, deliver, measure, and improve. Each risk is traced from the audience and the objective through to the activity, the metric, and the review.

| Section | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Assess | Current human risk, who is affected, and what the programme must change |
| Plan | Audiences, objectives, owners, and the activities chosen for each risk |
| Deliver | How warehouse, office, and remote staff receive the programme |
| Measure | Behaviour and outcome measures, kept separate from attendance |
| Improve | How results change the next cycle |
| Twelve-month calendar | The delivery and review schedule on one page |
| Reflection | What the design choices depend on |

## Risks the programme addresses

1. **Invoice and payment diversion.** Supplier bank-detail changes need a recorded callback before payment.
2. **Password reuse and account takeover.** Individual accounts move to multi-factor authentication, and reused credentials are removed from use.
3. **Customer data leaving work channels.** Staff use approved channels for regulated customer information, and confirmed loss is what the outcome target counts.

Warehouse staff are treated as a distinct audience. Many do not have an individual mailbox, so email click rates are not used as their result.

Planning choices in the report, including the training budget, the tool set, and the headcount split, are labelled as assumptions. They are separate from the facts given in the scenario.
